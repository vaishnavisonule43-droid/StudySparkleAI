Phishing

Learn how to identify and prevent phishing attacks.

📘 Introduction

Phishing is a type of cyber attack in which attackers use fake emails, messages, phone calls or websites to trick people into sharing personal information.

Attackers may try to steal passwords, bank details, OTPs or other sensitive information.

📖 1. Definition of Phishing

Phishing is a cyber attack in which an attacker pretends to be a trusted person or organization to steal sensitive information.

Example: Receiving a fake bank message asking you to click a link and enter your account details.

⚙️ 2. How Does Phishing Work?

Step 1: Fake Message

The attacker sends a fake email, SMS or social media message.

Step 2: Create Urgency

The message may say that your account will be blocked or that you have won a prize.

Step 3: Fake Link

The message contains a link to a fake website that looks like a genuine website.

Step 4: Steal Information

If the victim enters sensitive information, the attacker may collect it.

🧩 3. Types of Phishing

1. Email Phishing

Fake emails are sent to many people to steal their personal information.

Example: A fake email pretending to come from a bank.

2. Spear Phishing

This attack targets a specific person or organization using personalized messages.

Example: A fake email using an employee's name and job details.

3. Whaling

Whaling targets important people in an organization, such as senior executives.

Example: A fake message targeting a company director.

4. Smishing

Smishing is phishing carried out through SMS or text messages.

Example: A fake SMS asking you to update your bank account using a link.

5. Vishing

Vishing is phishing carried out through voice calls or phone conversations.

Example: A caller pretending to be bank support and asking for an OTP.

6. Clone Phishing

In clone phishing, an attacker copies a genuine message and replaces its original link or attachment with a malicious one.

Example: A copied delivery email containing a fake tracking link.

🚨 4. Warning Signs of Phishing

  • Unknown or suspicious sender address.
  • Messages asking for passwords or OTPs.
  • Unexpected links or attachments.
  • Spelling and grammar mistakes.
  • Urgent warnings or threats.
  • Offers that appear too good to be true.
  • Website addresses that do not match the real organization.

⚠️ 5. Effects of Phishing

  • Loss of personal information.
  • Unauthorized access to online accounts.
  • Financial loss and online fraud.
  • Identity theft.
  • Damage to personal or business reputation.
  • Malware infection through harmful attachments.

🔐 6. Prevention of Phishing

  • Do not click suspicious links.
  • Never share passwords or OTPs with callers or message senders.
  • Check the sender's email address carefully.
  • Visit official websites by typing their address yourself.
  • Use multi-factor authentication.
  • Keep your device and security software updated.
  • Report suspicious messages to the relevant organization.

💻 7. Example of a Phishing Attack

A person receives a message saying, "Your bank account will be blocked today. Click this link to update your details."

The link opens a fake website asking for the person's bank details and password.

Safety tip: Do not click the link. Open the bank's official app or website to check whether the message is genuine.

📊 8. Difference Between Phishing and Spam

Phishing Spam
Often tries to steal information or trick users. Usually refers to unwanted messages.
May contain fake login links or harmful attachments. May contain unwanted advertisements or promotions.
Can cause account theft and financial loss. Can be annoying, and some spam may also be harmful.

📝 9. Conclusion

Phishing is a common cyber attack that uses fake messages and websites to trick people. Checking links and senders carefully and never sharing passwords or OTPs helps protect us from phishing.
