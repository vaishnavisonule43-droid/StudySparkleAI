🧩 Expert Systems

Learn how expert systems use knowledge and rules to solve problems in a specific field.

Introduction to Expert Systems

An Expert System is an AI program that uses stored knowledge and rules to provide advice or solve problems in a particular area. It tries to perform tasks that normally require specialized human knowledge.

Example: A system that helps identify plant diseases.

1. What is an Expert System?

An Expert System is a computer program designed to solve specific problems using expert knowledge and logical rules. It provides recommendations or conclusions based on the information entered by the user.

Example: A system that suggests possible causes of a machine failure.

2. Main Components of an Expert System

  • Knowledge Base: Stores facts, information, and expert rules.
  • Inference Engine: Applies rules to the available information to reach a conclusion.
  • User Interface: Allows users to enter information and receive results.
  • Explanation Facility: Explains how the system reached a conclusion, when supported.
  • Knowledge Acquisition: Helps collect and update knowledge from experts and other sources.

3. How Does an Expert System Work?

  • Step 1: The user enters information about a problem.
  • Step 2: The system checks its stored knowledge.
  • Step 3: The inference engine applies relevant rules.
  • Step 4: The system produces a conclusion or recommendation.
  • Step 5: It may explain the result to the user.

4. Types of Expert Systems

  • Rule-Based System: Uses IF-THEN rules to reach conclusions.
  • Frame-Based System: Organizes knowledge into structured groups called frames.
  • Fuzzy Expert System: Uses degrees of truth instead of only true or false values.
  • Model-Based System: Uses a model of a system to help diagnose or solve problems.

5. Applications of Expert Systems

  • Medical decision support
  • Plant disease identification
  • Machine fault diagnosis
  • Banking and financial analysis
  • Technical troubleshooting
  • Equipment maintenance
  • Customer support

6. Advantages of Expert Systems

  • Provides quick advice in a specific field.
  • Can apply the same rules consistently.
  • Can preserve useful expert knowledge.
  • Can be available whenever the system is running.
  • Helps users make informed decisions.

7. Limitations of Expert Systems

  • Works mainly within its designed area.
  • May give incorrect results if its knowledge is incomplete.
  • Building and updating the knowledge base can be difficult.
  • May not handle unexpected situations well.
  • Cannot fully replace human expertise in every situation.

Real-Life Example

Suppose a farmer enters information about yellow leaves and spots on a plant. An agricultural expert system checks its stored rules and knowledge to suggest possible causes and recommend further checks. Its suggestion is not always a confirmed diagnosis.

Conclusion

Expert Systems are an important application of Artificial Intelligence. They use stored knowledge and rules to solve specific problems and provide useful recommendations in fields such as agriculture, healthcare, and engineering.
